Mention "case studies" and most small business owners picture a glossy corporate PDF, all charts and buzzwords — the sort of thing a big agency produces for another big agency. That image is doing you a disservice, because a case study is really just one piece of work, told properly, and it’s arguably the most persuasive thing a small business can put in front of a customer.

## What a case study actually is

Strip away the jargon and it’s a short, honest story: here’s what the customer wanted, here’s what we did, here’s how it turned out. That’s it. It can be three paragraphs and six photos. It doesn’t need statistics or a design budget. It needs to be *true* and *specific*.

The power is in the specificity. "We do great work" is a claim anyone can make. "A cramped Cheltenham ensuite, rebuilt as a walk-in wet room in two weeks — here’s the before, here’s the after, here’s what the customer said" is proof. One is marketing; the other is evidence.

## Why it beats almost everything else

When someone’s deciding whether to spend real money with you, they’re nervous. They’ve been let down before. A case study answers the question they’re actually asking — "can I trust these people with mine?" — by showing someone just like them who took the chance and was glad they did.

It works everywhere, too: on your website, in a reply to "can you send some examples?", in an email, as the backbone of a week of social posts. One job, written up once, keeps selling for years.

## What makes a good one

- **A clear before.** The problem, honestly described. No problem, no story.
- **The thinking, not just the result.** What you decided and why — this is what shows your judgement.
- **Real photos.** Before, during and after. The "during" shots are often the most persuasive, because nobody else bothers.
- **The customer’s words.** One honest line is worth a paragraph of your own.
- **Plain language.** Written like you talk, not like a brochure.

## You’ve already done the hard part

Here’s the thing most businesses miss: you’ve already done the work. The case study is just the record of it. Every good job you’ve finished is a case study you didn’t write down. The only question is whether anyone will ever get to see it.

## Start with your best one

Don’t try to document everything. Pick the one job you’re proudest of — the one you’d want a future customer to see — and tell that story properly. One strong case study outperforms a dozen thin ones.
