"How much does a website cost?" is a bit like asking what a vehicle costs — the honest answer runs from nearly nothing to more than a house, and the number only means something once you know what you actually need. Here’s a straight look at the real ranges in 2026, and what’s driving them.

## The honest price ranges

- **DIY builder (Wix, Squarespace, etc.):** roughly £10–£40 a month, plus your time. Fine for getting *something* up. The cost is the hours you spend and the ceiling you hit later.
- **A freelancer or small studio build:** often **£1,500–£6,000** for a proper small-business site, depending on size and how much is bespoke.
- **A larger agency:** **£6,000 upwards**, sometimes well into five figures, usually for bigger or more complex sites.

For most local Gloucestershire businesses, the middle band is where the sensible money sits — a site that’s properly designed and built, without paying for scale you don’t need.

## What actually drives the cost

The price isn’t really about "a website". It’s about these:

- **Size.** A sharp five-page site costs a fraction of a fifty-page one.
- **Bespoke vs template.** A design built around *your* brand costs more than a tweaked template — and usually earns it back by not looking like everyone else.
- **Content.** Who writes the words and sorts the photos? Good content is where sites succeed or fail, and it’s often the hidden cost.
- **Features.** Booking, payments, logins and the like all add up.
- **Who’s doing it.** A one-person freelancer, a studio, or a big agency price very differently for similar work.

## Where it’s worth spending

Not on animation and gimmicks. On the things that quietly decide whether the site earns its keep:

1. **Speed and mobile.** Most visitors are on a phone, and a slow site loses them before they read a word.
2. **Clear messaging and obvious next steps.** A beautiful site that doesn’t say what you do, or make enquiring easy, is an expensive brochure.
3. **Good content built in.** Your best work, your case studies, real photos — the stuff that actually convinces.
4. **Something you own and can grow.** Not rented space on a platform that owns your audience.

## Where you can save

- You rarely need every bell and whistle at launch. Start with a strong core and add later.
- You don’t need fifty pages. You need the right five or ten, done well.
- You don’t need to rebuild from scratch if a sharp refresh would do — sometimes it would.

## The real question

It isn’t "what’s the cheapest site I can get." It’s "what will this site *earn* me?" A site that turns visitors into enquiries pays for itself many times over; one that just sits there is a cost at any price. Judge the spend against the return, not the sticker.
