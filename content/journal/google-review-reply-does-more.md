Most businesses treat a review as the end of the conversation. The customer has spoken, the stars are logged, move on. But the reply is where the real work happens — because the reply isn’t for the person who wrote the review. It’s for every future customer reading it to decide whether to trust you.

## Who’s actually reading your replies

When someone’s weighing you up, they don’t just read the reviews — they read how you *respond* to them. A business that replies, thoughtfully and in its own voice, reads as one that cares and is paying attention. A wall of five-star reviews with no replies feels oddly cold by comparison.

And there’s a quieter benefit: Google has said that responding to reviews is part of a well-managed profile, and active profiles tend to do better in local search. You’re writing for customers first — but it doesn’t hurt with the algorithm.

## Replying to a good review

Don’t just say "Thanks!". A good reply does three small jobs:

1. **Thank them by name** — it reads as real, not automated.
2. **Mention the specific thing** they came in for — it subtly signals to future readers what you do well.
3. **Leave the door open** — "see you next time" or "give us a shout for the next one."

*"Thanks so much, Sarah — really glad you’re pleased with the wet room, and that the underfloor heating’s already earning its keep. Any trouble at all, you know where we are."*

That reply isn’t for Sarah. It’s for the next person searching "wet room fitter Cheltenham" who reads it.

## Replying to a bad one

This is where businesses either win or lose trust in public. The rules:

- **Reply calmly, and quickly.** Silence looks like guilt.
- **Don’t argue the facts in public.** Acknowledge, apologise where fair, and move it offline: "I’d like to put this right — could you drop us a line so we can look into it?"
- **Stay human.** Future readers forgive a mistake handled gracefully far more than they forgive defensiveness.

A measured reply to a harsh review often does *more* for your reputation than another five-star would. It shows you under pressure — and people are watching for exactly that.

## Make it a habit, not a scramble

Reviews come in unpredictably, which is why replies get forgotten. The fix is simple: a standing reminder to check once or twice a week, and a couple of flexible openings you can adapt rather than writing from scratch each time.
