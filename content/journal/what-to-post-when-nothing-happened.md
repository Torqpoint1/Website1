The most common reason good businesses go quiet online isn’t laziness. It’s the belief that you need *news* to post — a launch, an award, a big reveal. You don’t. The ordinary work you do every day is far more interesting to a potential customer than you think, because they’ve never seen it.

## Why ‘boring’ beats ‘announcement’

Nobody follows a local business for its press releases. They follow for a look behind the curtain — how things are made, who makes them, what good looks like. The stuff you find mundane is exactly the stuff a customer finds reassuring. Familiarity is the whole point.

## Fifteen posts hiding in a normal week

You almost certainly have the raw material for a month sitting in your camera roll already. For example:

- A job, project or order **before and after**
- A close-up of a **detail** most people would never notice
- The **tools or ingredients** you use and why they’re the good ones
- A **question a customer asked** this week, answered
- A **myth** in your trade, politely corrected
- **Behind the scenes** — the prep, the setup, the early start
- A **regular** or repeat customer (with their blessing)
- The **view from where you work** on a good morning
- A **mistake you see all the time** and how to avoid it
- **This vs that** — two options compared honestly
- A **short how-to** for something people get wrong
- The **story behind** why you started
- **Seasonal** — what changes in your work this time of year
- A **genuine thank-you** after a good job
- **What you’re reading, using or recommending** right now

None of those require anything to have *happened*. They require you to notice what you already do.

## The format matters less than the habit

A phone photo with two honest sentences will outperform a polished graphic you only manage once a quarter. Consistency is what the platforms reward and what customers remember. Three ordinary posts a month beats one perfect post a season, every time.

## A simple system

Keep a note on your phone called "post ideas". When something small and interesting happens — a good question, a satisfying finish, a nice bit of light — take the photo there and then and jot the line. By the end of the week you’ve got your content without ever sitting down to "do marketing."
