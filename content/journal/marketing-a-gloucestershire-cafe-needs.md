Run a café and the marketing advice comes at you from every direction: be on TikTok, run competitions, start a loyalty app, post three times a day. Most of it is noise. A good independent café in Gloucestershire needs three things, and the rest is optional.

## 1. A Google profile that makes someone cross the road

When a visitor to Cheltenham types "coffee near me", Google shows a map and three listings. That panel — not your Instagram, not your website — is where the decision gets made. If your photos are dark, your opening hours are wrong, or your last update was two years ago, the person scrolls to the café that looks open and cared for.

Keeping it current is the single highest-return thing you can do. Recent photos, correct hours (especially bank holidays), a few words about what you do well. It’s free, and it’s shown at the exact moment someone is deciding where to spend their money.

## 2. Photos that make people hungry

People eat with their eyes long before they taste anything. One good photo of the actual thing you sell — the flat white, the sausage roll still steaming, the corner table in the morning light — does more than a paragraph of copy ever will.

You don’t need a photographer. A modern phone, a window, and thirty seconds before the first customer arrives is enough. The trick is doing it regularly, so the feed and the profile always look like *this week*, not last spring.

## 3. A reason to come back

Most of a café’s money isn’t in the first visit — it’s in the hundredth. The regulars are the business. Anything that gently reminds people you exist earns its keep: a simple email when the autumn menu lands, a Google post about the new beans, a sign that there’s something worth coming in for.

## What you can safely ignore

- **Chasing viral videos.** One in ten thousand works; the rest is time you don’t have.
- **Being on every platform.** Pick the one your actual customers use and do it properly.
- **Discount-chasing.** Training people to only come in when it’s cheap is a hard habit to undo.

## The honest version

A café lives or dies on the product and the welcome — and no marketing fixes a flat white nobody wants. But assuming the coffee’s good, the businesses that stay busy are the ones that look alive online and give people a reason to return. That’s it. That’s the list.
