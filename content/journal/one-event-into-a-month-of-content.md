An event is a strange kind of marketing asset. Months of planning, one intense day, and then it’s gone — the guests leave, the room is cleared, and the whole thing lives on in a few phones. But a single well-run event holds enough material to keep a business visible for weeks, if someone captures it with that in mind.

## The mindset shift: capture, then publish later

The mistake is treating the event as a thing to post *about on the day*. The better approach is to treat it as a shoot — gather far more than you need, while it’s happening, and ration it out afterwards when your feed would otherwise be quiet.

One Saturday wedding or one sold-out supper club can comfortably feed a month if you capture the full arc, not just the finished room.

## What to capture (beyond the obvious ‘after’ shot)

- **The build-up** — the empty space, the setup, the detail going in
- **The small details** — the thing you agonised over that guests half-noticed
- **The people** (with permission) — reactions, not just arrangements
- **A short clip** of the moment it all came together
- **The morning-after** — honest, unglamorous, oddly popular
- **A number or two** — covers served, miles travelled, hours on site

## How one event becomes a month

Here’s a realistic breakdown from a single day:

1. **Week one** — the reveal: the best finished shot, with the story of what the client wanted.
2. **Week two** — a detail post: one element, close up, and why it mattered.
3. **Week three** — behind the scenes: the setup, the graft nobody sees.
4. **Week four** — a short testimonial or a thank-you, pointing gently at "booking for next season".

Alongside that, the same event gives you an **email** to your past guests ("here’s what we did last weekend — dates for yours are open") and, if it’s a strong one, a **case study** on your website that quietly sells every enquiry that follows.

## The capture plan is the whole trick

None of this works if you remember at 11pm that you meant to take photos. Decide beforehand who’s responsible for grabbing the shots, keep a short list on your phone, and treat it as part of running the event — because it is.
