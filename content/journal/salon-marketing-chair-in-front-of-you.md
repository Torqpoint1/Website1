Walk into most salons and the marketing worry is the same: how do we get *new* clients? It’s the wrong first question. The most valuable person for a salon isn’t the stranger who might come once — it’s the client already in the chair, who could come back eight times a year for a decade. Keep them, and the maths of the business changes completely.

## The number that should keep you up at night

A new client is expensive to win and uncertain to keep. An existing one is cheap to keep and worth a fortune over time. A client who visits every six weeks is worth hundreds a year, thousands over the relationship — and all it takes to lose them is forgetting to get them back through the door.

So before spending a penny on attracting strangers, it’s worth asking: are we doing everything to keep the people who already chose us?

## Three quiet wins most salons miss

**1. Rebook before they leave.** The single highest-return habit in the business. A client who walks out with the next appointment booked is a client who comes back. One who leaves meaning to "ring and sort it" is a coin toss. Build it into the goodbye, every time.

**2. Remember them.** A quick note on preferences, the colour formula, the thing they mentioned last time. Clients stay where they feel known, and that feeling is almost entirely in the small details you recall.

**3. Stay in touch between visits.** A short, friendly email — a seasonal look, a reminder it’s been a while, a new treatment worth trying. It costs nothing and it lands in front of people who already trust you. That’s the warmest list you’ll ever have.

## Where social fits (and where it doesn’t)

Great hair and nails photograph beautifully, and a strong feed absolutely helps win new clients and show your range. Post the work — it’s some of the best content any business gets handed for free. But don’t mistake a busy feed for a full column. The feed brings people in; the rebook and the follow-up keep them.

## The honest order of priorities

1. **Rebook** the client in the chair.
2. **Remember** them so they feel known.
3. **Reach out** between visits so you stay top of mind.
4. **Then** worry about attracting new faces.

Get the first three right and the fourth gets easier anyway, because happy regulars are the best referral engine there is.
