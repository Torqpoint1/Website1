The best-looking jobs often end up with the worst photos — two quick snaps at handover, in bad light, with the homeowner’s washing in the background. It’s not a camera problem. It’s a timing problem.

Here’s the shot list we give every client. It adds about two minutes to a job.

## Before you start (3 shots)

The “before” is the most valuable photo you’ll ever take, and the one most often forgotten.

1. **The wide shot** — stand in the doorway or the corner and get the whole space in
2. **The problem** — the cracked tiles, the rotten fascia, the waterlogged lawn
3. **The same angle you’ll use for the “after”** — pick a spot you can find again

## During the job (4–5 shots)

This is what homeowners never see and what sets you apart from the firm that cuts corners.

- **First fix** — pipework, cabling, joists, drainage. Proof of what’s behind the finish
- **The prep** — tanking, levelling, the membrane going down
- **One detail shot** — a joint, a cut, a mitre. The thing you’re quietly proud of
- **The team at work** — hands and tools rather than faces, if the lads are camera-shy

## At handover (4 shots)

Tidy up first. Five minutes of clearing dust sheets and bins makes more difference than any camera.

1. **The “after” from your “before” spot** — same angle, same height
2. **Two detail shots** — close enough to see the quality
3. **One wide in natural light** — open the blinds, switch off the overhead lights if they’re harsh

## Four habits that fix most bad photos

- **Hold the phone upright for social, sideways for your website** — or take both
- **Wipe the lens.** It’s been in your pocket all day
- **Stand still and tap to focus** on the part that matters
- **Don’t zoom** — step closer instead

## Then send it all, straight away

Photos taken and never sent are as good as photos never taken. Drop them in a WhatsApp chat the same day with a two-minute voice note: what the customer wanted, what was tricky, what you’re pleased with. That’s everything needed to turn one job into proof for the next.
