Ask the internet how often to post and you’ll get a different number from every corner — daily, twice a day, five times a week, "as much as possible." For a small business with actual work to do, most of that advice is written by people whose full-time job *is* posting. Here’s the honest version.

## Consistency beats volume. Every time.

The single biggest factor isn’t how *much* you post — it’s whether you keep it up. A business that posts twice a week for a year will beat one that posts daily for a fortnight and then vanishes. The platforms reward reliability, and so do customers: a steady presence reads as a steady business.

A quiet feed, on the other hand, quietly worries people. If your last post was in spring, a customer wonders whether you’re still going.

## A realistic cadence that works

For most local businesses, the sweet spot is **one to three posts a week**. That’s frequent enough to stay visible, rare enough to keep each post worth looking at, and — crucially — achievable without it taking over your week.

- **Once a week** is the floor. Below that, you’re effectively dormant.
- **Two to three a week** is the comfortable, sustainable target for most.
- **Daily** is only worth it if your business genuinely generates that much worth seeing, and you have someone whose job it is to do it.

## Why more isn’t better

Post too often with nothing to say and two things happen: the quality drops, and you burn out. A feed of filler trains people to scroll past you. One good post a week that someone actually stops for is worth more than seven they ignore.

## Pick a rhythm and protect it

The businesses that get this right don’t rely on inspiration. They batch. An hour every week or two, or a month captured in one go, turns "I should post something" into a system that runs whether you feel like it or not.

- Decide your days (say, Tuesday and Friday).
- Capture the raw material as you go, not the night before.
- Batch the writing and scheduling so the week looks after itself.

## The real answer

How often should you post? Often enough to stay in people’s minds, rarely enough that each one earns its place, and — above all — regularly enough that you never go silent. For most Gloucestershire businesses, that’s a couple of times a week, kept up all year.
