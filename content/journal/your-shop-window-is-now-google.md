High streets used to live or die on the shop window — the display that stopped someone mid-stride and pulled them in. That window still matters. But for most businesses, there’s now an earlier one, and it’s on a phone: the Google listing people glance at *before* they decide whether to come to you at all.

## The first impression moved

Here’s the sequence for a modern customer. They hear your name, or they search for what they need. They tap your listing. In about three seconds they form a view — open or closed, busy or dead, cared-for or neglected — and that view decides whether they bother with the real shop window at all.

If the digital window is dark, half the footfall never arrives to see the good one.

## What people actually see first

Open your own listing on a phone, as a stranger would, and look honestly:

- **The photos.** Are they recent, bright, and genuinely yours — or a grainy shot from years ago and whatever passers-by uploaded?
- **The stars and the number of reviews.** This is the trust signal people scan first.
- **The hours.** Do they say you’re open right now? Are the holiday hours right?
- **The category and description.** Does it instantly say what you are, or make people guess?
- **The replies.** Do you respond to reviews, or is it a one-way wall?

Most businesses have never looked at their own listing this way. Do it today — you’ll almost certainly find something that’s quietly costing you visits.

## Dressing the window properly

- **Lead with your best, current photos.** Swap them with the seasons, like a real display.
- **Keep hours ruthlessly accurate.** The fastest way to lose trust is a wrong "closed."
- **Write the description for a human.** Plain words, what you sell, who it’s for.
- **Keep it alive** with the occasional post — a new arrival, a seasonal line, a recent job.

## It’s free, which is exactly why it’s neglected

Nobody sends you an invoice for a dusty Google listing, so it slides. But it’s working — or failing — for you every single day, in front of people at the precise moment they’re deciding. A tidy, current profile is the cheapest uplift in local marketing, and most businesses leave it on the table.
