Every few years someone declares email dead, usually someone selling something shinier. And every year email quietly keeps outperforming almost everything else for small businesses. The reason is simple, and it comes down to one word: ownership.

## The fundamental difference

When you post on social media, a platform decides how many of your followers actually see it — often a small fraction. You built that audience; you don’t control access to it. Change the algorithm, and your reach can halve overnight with no warning.

Email is the opposite. Your list is *yours*. When you send, it lands in the inbox of every person who chose to hear from you. No gatekeeper, no pay-to-reach, no algorithm deciding you’re not worth showing today. You own the relationship outright.

## Three reasons it still wins

**1. It reaches people who already chose you.** A social follower might be a bot, a competitor, or someone who tapped follow once and forgot. An email subscriber actively handed you their address. That’s a warmer audience by a mile.

**2. It’s built for action, not scrolling.** People check email with intent. A clear message with one obvious next step — book, buy, reply — converts far better than the same message lost in a feed.

**3. It’s absurdly cheap.** No ad spend, no per-lead fee. The cost of reaching a few hundred past customers is essentially nothing, and those are the people most likely to buy again.

## "But I don’t have a list"

Nearly everyone starts here, and it’s fine. You don’t need thousands. A list of a hundred past customers who like you is worth more than ten thousand strangers. Start small and build the habit:

- **Ask.** At the point of sale, after a job, on the website — "want the occasional update? Pop your email here."
- **Give a reason.** First to know about dates, offers, new work.
- **Keep it clean and consenting.** Only people who opted in. Quality over quantity, always.

## How often, and what to say

For most local businesses, monthly or even quarterly is plenty — often enough to be remembered, rare enough to be welcome. And you don’t need news. The latest project, a seasonal nudge, a genuinely useful tip, a quiet "we’ve got space this month." The goal is to stay warmly in mind, so when the need comes up, you’re the name they already have.

## The quiet workhorse

Social media is the shop window that pulls strangers past. Email is the regular who comes back because you remembered them. You want both — but if you can only build one properly, build the one you own.
