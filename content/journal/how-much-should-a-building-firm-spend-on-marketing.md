Ask ten people and you’ll get ten percentages. The truth is that a sensible marketing budget for a trade business starts with a much more useful number: what one extra job is worth to you.

## Start with the value of a job

Take your typical job value and your margin on it. If an average job is £12,000 and you clear 25%, one extra job is worth about £3,000 in profit.

Now ask: how many *extra* jobs a year would make a real difference? For a firm with a team and a couple of vans, two or three more good jobs a year is often the gap between a tight year and a comfortable one.

## Then set the budget against that

If two extra jobs are worth £6,000 in profit, spending £5,000 a year to reliably win them is a good trade — especially if what you’re paying for keeps working in year two. Spending £5,000 on something that can’t be traced to a single job is not.

As a rough sense-check, many established small firms land somewhere around a few percent of turnover. But treat that as a sanity check, not a target.

## Spend it on things you keep

The biggest mistake isn’t spending too much or too little. It’s spending every year on things that reset — ads that stop the day you stop paying, leads shared with four other firms.

A better split for most established trades:

- **Your Google Business Profile** — kept active every month. The cheapest, highest-intent visibility there is
- **Finished jobs written up** — the proof that wins the quote when a homeowner is comparing
- **Your own website** — where it all lives, and where enquiries come only to you
- **Directories, if they earn their keep** — measured on cost per job won, reviewed at renewal

## Measure one thing

Ask every new enquiry how they found you, and write it down. After six months you’ll know exactly what’s working — which beats any percentage.
