## The job

The back of a 1930s semi in Charlton Kings, with a leaking lean-to conservatory and a cracked patio. Cleeve Build took it down, dug and poured the footings, built up in block, put a steel in over a six-metre opening, and finished with a flat roof, a roof lantern and anthracite bifolds onto a new porcelain patio. Twelve weeks, building control signed off, family cooking in it by the summer.

## The problem

Extensions are the biggest spend most homeowners will make on their house, and they take months to decide. Cleeve Build's best advert was always the finished job — but by the time it was finished, nobody had kept a record of how it got there. Twelve weeks of good work, and two photos at the end.

## What twelve weeks became

On Engine, the job was captured as it happened rather than remembered afterwards. Photos at each stage, a voice note every Friday, and from that:

- **A case study page** telling the build from footings to handover
- **A before, during and after set**, with the steel going in as the centrepiece
- **Monthly social posts** following the build, so local homeowners watched it go up
- **Google Business posts** at each milestone, keeping the profile active through the whole job
- **A review request**, timed for the week the family moved back into the kitchen

### Sample — social post (week six)

> Steel's in. Six metres of opening where a leaky lean-to used to be. Next week: roof joists. Following this one from footings to bifolds — Charlton Kings, 1930s semi.

### Sample — case study opening

> The owners wanted two things: a kitchen they could all fit in, and to never see that conservatory again. Twelve weeks later, they got both.

## Why it works

Homeowners planning an extension want reassurance more than anything — that the builder turns up, keeps a tidy site and finishes what they start. A build told week by week proves all three before they've picked up the phone.
